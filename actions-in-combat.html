Actions in Combat

Players have 2AP per turn and each of the below costs 1AP. Players can choose to do the same action twice if they wish.

- Test for an ability

- Attack

  • Players can make an attack that is designed hit a specific area. IF they can describe how it will cause a knock-on effect that will cause secondary DMG, it causes 2 pts DMG.
    • E.G. If an enemy is standing on a table and the player targets the legs to sweep them, the enemy falls off and takes a 2nd pt of DMG.

- Move

  • Players get their total movement by selecting this and can split the movement. i.e. move, take action, finish movement

- Evade

  • Until the start of your next turn, you can Test 1d6 when you are successfully hit by an enemy.
  • If your Test is successful, you evade the attack and do not take damage.

- Focus

  • The next time you attack, your Test is successful on a roll of 4, 5, or 6, increasing your chances of hitting the enemy.
  • Your Focus Action remains in effect until you choose to attack or until the end of combat, so the Action can carry over to other turns.
  • There is no benefit to stacking Focus Actions.

- Suppressing Fire

  • Lets you make an attack at Disadvantage every time an enemy enters the area you’re suppressing.

  • Suppressed Area must be a small defined area

    • Enemies come from behind a specific wall or shoot from a specific window.

  • Can also be used in melee range.

- Cover

  • All enemy attacks from one direction gain Disadvantage
  • There needs to be some physical object to hide behind - Can't just use out in the open
    • Has to be behind something big enough that the PC can reasonably hide behind - big rock, other person, car, etc...
  • If PC is behind full cover (Solid wall, etc...), there is no need to use this Action

The Game Master will usually announce when Combat is about to begin by requesting an Initiative Test. Survivors will roll a standard 2d6 Test and add up the total of their rolls. Likewise, the Game Master will roll Initiative for any enemy combatants the Survivors will be facing. The highest roller will have the first turn in the Initiative Order, followed by the next highest, and so on. If there is a tie between a Survivor and an enemy, the Survivor always goes first. If there is a tie between two or more Survivors, the tying Survivors reroll for that position until the tie is broken.

Combat is strictly turn-based to keep things running smoothly. A round of combat begins at the top of the Initiative Order and ends at the bottom. The next round of combat then begins, starting again at the top of the Initiative Order. This continues until the Survivors have defeated their enemies, completed some objective determined by the Game Master, or until the Survivors have been killed or routed. While it can take some time to get through an entire round of combat, in-game, a full round of combat is equal to roughly five or six seconds.

During your turn in the Initiative Order, you have two Actions. You can choose to move, attack, and generally not be useless with these Actions. If you move, that is one Action. If you attack, that is one Action. You can use both Actions to move, or use both Actions to Attack if you wish. Other Actions include sheathing or unsheathing a weapon, grabbing an item, and giving an item to an ally.

Movement is defined by announcing your intentions to move from one location to another; the Game Master determines whether you can cover enough ground to get there with that Action. A good rule of thumb is to assume your character can move approximately 25 feet in-game with a single Action, so long as there isn’t anything hindering their movement. If you are using a combat grid, 25 feet is 5 squares.
